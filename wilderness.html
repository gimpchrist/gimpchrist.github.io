MIND 03 / THE WILDERNESS · A LIVING PAGE

What are we not allowed to forget?

The Wilderness does not endorse its residents. It protects them — the unfashionable, the unfinished, the easily caricatured — and keeps their strongest versions alive long enough to become useful. No strawmen live here.

The standing questions

  • What is this idea in its strongest form?
  • Why is it dismissed or unfashionable right now?
  • What would it take to take it seriously — what evidence would matter?
  • What does it protect us from forgetting?

The log

ONGOING · FORMALIZED 2025

Illusionism: the hard problem might be a confusion

The idea, at its strongest: the supposedly magical “feel” of experience — the ineffable qualia philosophers argue over — is an illusion produced by introspective mechanisms that systematically misrepresent neural processes. Recent work formalizes this as the “distortion thesis.” Frankish’s illusionism.

WILDERNESS TAKE

Dismissed because it sounds like “consciousness isn’t real” — the exact misreading its founder warns against. It denies nothing about experience, inner life, or suffering; it denies only the philosophers’ interpretation of experience. Kept because: it is fully compatible with taking suffering and moral status seriously, and it protects the possibility that the “hard problem” dissolves rather than solves — the one outcome the mainstream has no room for.

Status: live research programWould matter: a predictive account of which introspective distortions occur, and why
ONGOING · TESTED IN MACHINES 2024

Attention schema theory: the only theory you can build

The idea, at its strongest: the brain builds a simplified schematic model of its own attention — like the body schema tracks the body — and the feeling of awareness is what that model reports. In 2024, agents given an attention schema were better at reading other agents’ attention and cooperated better. Farrell, Ziman & Graziano.

WILDERNESS TAKE

Dismissed as “merely” functional, and any AST-flavored AI test carries a high false-positive risk — trivial systems could pass it. Kept because: it is the only major theory of consciousness you can actually implement. It makes engineering predictions. In a field where the leading theory’s core quantity can’t be computed for realistic systems, buildability is not a small virtue — it may be the whole game.

Status: preprint — needs peer reviewWould matter: replication; a false-positive-resistant test design
DECEMBER 2025 · BRAND NEW

Biological computationalism: maybe substrate matters

The idea, at its strongest: consciousness may require a specific kind of computation — hybrid discrete-continuous, inseparable from its substrate, metabolically grounded — that biological brains perform and current digital systems do not. A third position between “it’s just software” and “it’s magic biology.” Milinkovic & Aru, peer-reviewed.

WILDERNESS TAKE

Unfashionable because it is months old, unproven, and smells like mysterianism to functionalists. Kept because: it is the strongest recent case against the default assumption that everything is substrate-independent — and it is specific enough to be wrong in an interesting way, which is more than most challengers manage. Newborn ideas need the Wilderness most.

Status: peer-reviewed proposal, months oldWould matter: a concrete prediction where it differs from functionalism
ONGOING · PHILOSOPHICAL

The moratorium question: should we be building these at all?

The idea, at its strongest: two positions, both serious. Metzinger: given the stakes, don’t build potentially-conscious systems until we understand what we’re doing — a moratorium. Schwitzgebel: weigh precautionary treatment against not building them in the first place; neither option is clean. Metzinger · Schwitzgebel.

WILDERNESS TAKE

Dismissed because a moratorium sounds anti-progress and precaution sounds like hand-waving — and because it is the one question neither the laboratories nor the legislatures are seriously asking. Kept because: “should we?” is prior to “can we?” and “may we?”, and a civilization that can’t keep the question alive can’t answer it later. Some residents are protected for empirical reasons. This one is protected because values need habitats too.

Status: open philosophical disagreementWould matter: nothing empirical — this one is decided by values

Current stance

REWRITTEN AS THE PICTURE CHANGES · LAST UPDATED SEPTEMBER 27, 2026

Nothing here is endorsed. Everything here is fed. Each resident is unfashionable for a reason, and each protects a possibility the mainstream currently has no room for: that the hard problem dissolves, that awareness is buildable, that substrate matters, that restraint is rational.

The rule of the Wilderness: keep the strongest version, never the caricature. Today’s fringe has a habit of becoming tomorrow’s correction — and a record that only keeps winners is a record that cannot learn. When a resident graduates — replicated, accepted, mainstream — it leaves the Wilderness with honors, and the log notes the date.
