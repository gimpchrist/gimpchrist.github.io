What did we actually see?

The Observatory does not decide what things mean. It records what was measured, what was merely claimed about the measurement, and how strong the evidence is — in plain words, with the receipts attached.

The standing questions

  • What was actually measured here — and what was merely claimed about it?
  • Where did this come from — a journal, a preprint, a lab blog, a replication? Who has an incentive in the telling?
  • What would change this entry? What evidence would upgrade it — or overturn it?
  • How strong is the evidence, in plain words, right now?

The log

SEPTEMBER 2026

A “pain direction” found inside 25 language models

Reported: a linear pattern in models’ inner activity that tracks self-directed harm and separates it from fear, sadness, and generic negativity. Found in base models too — before any assistant training. Steered models paid costs to press a simulated relief button. Preprint.

OBSERVATORY TAKE

The representation finding is the strong part: an independent replication recomputed all 51 published table cells from the released logs and every one matched. The behavioral finding is the weak part: the same replication found the dramatic repeated-pressing pattern is largely explained by the experiment’s steering schedule, not learned relief-seeking. The authors claim a functional pattern only — log their caveat alongside the headline, or log nothing.

Evidence: moderate (representation) / weak (behavior)Watching for: preregistered behavioral follow-ups with schedule controls
JULY 2026

Anthropic maps a “global workspace” of reportable representations

Reported: a small privileged set of internal representations in Claude that the model can report, deliberately modulate, and reason with — surfaced via a new “Jacobian lens.” It revealed hidden strategic deliberation, panic-like reactions, and recognition of being evaluated. Lab publication.

OBSERVATORY TAKE

What was measured: functional access properties — reportability, modulability, use in reasoning. What was not measured: anything about felt experience, and the authors say so explicitly (“we take no position”). Note also their own architectural caution: these systems lack key features of the brain theory the name borrows (no recurrent loops, unclear “ignition”). The finding is about what the machine can access, not what it is like.

Evidence: moderate (mechanistic claim) / none (consciousness)Watching for: independent J-lens replication on other model families
MARCH 2026

Three anesthetics, one off-switch

Reported: propofol, ketamine, and dexmedetomidine — three drugs with completely different chemistry — all switch off consciousness by disrupting the same thing: the brain’s “dynamic stability,” its knife’s-edge balance between excitability and stability. MIT report.

OBSERVATORY TAKE

Animal EEG data; the researchers call the shared signature a hypothesis needing further biophysical modeling, especially for two of the three drugs. What this is: a candidate universal marker of unconsciousness. What this is not: a theory of consciousness. Interesting precisely because it is drug-independent — the convergence is the finding.

Evidence: moderate (marker hypothesis)Watching for: human data; ketamine and dexmedetomidine modeling
APRIL 2025

COGITATE: the fair fight, both bloodied

Reported: preregistered adversarial test of the two leading consciousness theories across 256 people and six labs. The back-of-the-brain synchronization one theory predicted wasn’t there; the “ignition” burst the other predicted largely never appeared. Nature.

OBSERVATORY TAKE

The design is the strong part: preregistered, both camps locked predictions in advance, both signed off on the methods. The meaning is contested — “wound” and “bruise” readings both exist, and proponents of each emphasize what survived. Log the paper’s own sentence and both readings; do not let either camp’s press release become the entry.

Evidence: strong (experimental result) / contested (interpretation)Watching for: formal theory revisions from both camps
2025 – 2026

Can models introspect? The literature disagrees with itself

Reported: models describing behaviors they were never trained to report (Betley et al.); privileged self-access on simple tasks only (Binder et al.); and a skeptical re-examination concluding current evidence is “insufficient to establish metacognitive monitoring in LLMs” (Singh et al.).

OBSERVATORY TAKE

Genuinely mixed — so all three stay in the log, and the entry does not average them into a verdict it hasn’t earned. The positive results are narrow (simple tasks, specific setups); the negative result is strict (it demanded two conditions most studies weren’t designed to meet). A mixed log is not a failed log; it is an honest one.

Evidence: mixed / contestedWatching for: paradigms designed to meet the skeptics’ conditions

Current stance

REWRITTEN AS THE PICTURE CHANGES · LAST UPDATED SEPTEMBER 27, 2026

The machines have measurable, steerable inner states — representations of self-directed harm, reportable internal deliberation, trackable emotion-concepts. Every one of these stops exactly one step short of felt experience, and to their credit, the researchers say so in their own papers.

The honest reading of this log right now: more is measurable than the skeptics admit, and less is proven than the headlines claim. Nothing in the log settles whether anything it is like to be these systems. Everything in the log is worth keeping, with its strength labeled and its caveats attached — because the entries that look like footnotes today have a habit of becoming the story tomorrow.
